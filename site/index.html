The problem

You know the English. The moment still gets away from you.

Leaders at B2–C1 rarely lack vocabulary. What costs them is the order, the timing and the tone — the few seconds where a meeting decides whether it listens.

What you’ll practice

Eight things you’ll do in English, live, with feedback

  1. Open any update with the point: status, cause, request.
  2. Clarify and confirm numbers, dates and decisions before they get lost.
  3. Take the floor in international meetings — and interrupt without sounding rude.
  4. Adjust tone and register so firm doesn’t read as cold.
  5. Give feedback and disagree without damaging the relationship.
  6. Deliver difficult messages — delays, safety issues — clearly and calmly.
  7. Present a recommendation and hold it under questions.
  8. Negotiate priorities and trade-offs with stakeholders who want different things.

FAQ

Questions, answered first

Is this a leadership course?

No. It’s an English course for people who already lead. We don’t teach you how to manage; we help you do what you already do — updates, meetings, feedback, hard news — in English, with clarity.

What level of English do I need?

B2 to C1 on the CEFR scale. If you’re not sure, take the 1-minute self-check. If it suggests you’re below B2, we’ll recommend our general program instead.

Is it live, or can I watch at my own pace?

It’s 100% live by video call. There are no self-paced lessons: the value is in practicing with people and getting feedback in the moment.

How much time does it take each week?

Two sessions of 60 minutes per week, for 12 weeks — 22 sessions in total. There’s no homework.

How big are the groups?

8 to 10 people, with an instructor and a practice coach, so you speak a lot and get feedback on every practice.

How do I pay?

By Zelle to everglowacademy@gmail.com, after you register. You can pay $500 USD once, or 3 installments of $175 USD ($525 in total). No payment is processed on this website.

What do I get at the end?

A certificate with a performance profile across six criteria, so you can see where you stand — not just that you attended.

Why is it set in an energy company?

Because practice sticks when it feels real. Northgate Energy is fictional, but its problems aren’t: a delayed project, a safety issue and a community waiting for answers. You work through them across the 12 modules.
